# Does Family History Predict Osteoporosis Risk?

A personal data project exploring whether family history of osteoporosis 
is associated with a higher diagnosis rate and what role lifestyle 
factors like calcium, vitamin D, and physical activity might play.

## Why
My grandmother, on my mother's side, has osteoporosis. We didn't know 
until she started losing her ability to move freely — her bones were 
quietly breaking down for years before anyone recognized what was 
happening. That experience left me wondering: now that we know it runs 
in our family, are my siblings, cousins, and I at greater risk? And if 
family history really does matter, what could we be doing differently, 
starting now, to lower that risk?

## Question
In this dataset, do individuals with a family history of osteoporosis 
have a higher rate of osteoporosis diagnosis than those without one?

**Smaller questions:**
- Does the gap hold up across age groups, or is it mainly driven by older individuals?
- Among those with family history, does sufficient calcium/vitamin D intake and regular physical activity lower the diagnosis rate?

## Data
[Lifestyle Factors Influencing Osteoporosis](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/amitvkulkarni/lifestyle-factors-influencing-osteoporosis) 
(Kaggle) — 1,959 rows, 16 columns, one row per individual.
